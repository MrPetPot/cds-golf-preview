Editorial guide · Costa del Sol

01 · The method

How each development's position is worked out: what the golf setting contributes, what the development contributes, and where editorial judgement comes in.

The matrix

100 points · ten criteria · two blocks.

The golf setting is worth up to 60 points; the development's own characteristics, up to 40. Six criteria, worth a maximum of 65 points between them, are calculated from the recorded data; the other four, worth up to 35, call for an editorial judgement whose reasoning appears on every profile. Once the data and those judgements are fixed, the total and the order follow on their own.

Block A · Golf setting

60 points · The heart of the ranking

This measures the quality and the proximity of the courses, how many lie within a useful distance, and what access they actually grant. Living beside a course and being able to book a tee time are not the same thing.

A1
Benchmark course
★★★★ = 15 · ★★★ = 10 · ★★ = 5

The quality of the nearest course rated ★★ or better. Its rating comes from the guide's own course scale.

15
A2
★★★★ courses within 15 minutes · weighted by access
≥3 = 12 · ≥2 = 8 · ≥1 = 5 · >0 = 2 · 0 = 0
12
A3
★★★ or ★★★★ courses within 15 minutes
≥6 = 10 · ≥4 = 7 · ≥2 = 4 · ≥1 = 2 · >0 = 1

Same coefficient. A ★★★★ course counts in both criteria: these are two different weightings, not the same point awarded twice.

10
A4
Driving time to the benchmark course
≤3 min = 8 · 4–6 = 6 · 7–10 = 4 · 11–15 = 2

Driving time measured with OSRM, from the development's coordinate to the club entrance.

8
A5
Golf integration
Resort ★★★★ = 10 · ★★★ = 7 · ★★ = 4 · On foot = 5 · Own course not yet open = 0

Editorial. A verified link between development and course: inside a resort, or directly on the course. An announced course that is not yet open earns nothing.

10
A6
Road connections
Excellent = 5 · Good = 3 · Limited = 1

Editorial. How easy the journey to the surrounding courses really is, rather than distance as the crow flies.

5

The 'benchmark course' is the course rated ★★ or better with the shortest recorded driving time. A par-3 or an executive course rated ★ may appear on the profile, but it does not decide A1 or A4. The star ratings are documented under The courses.

Block B · The development

40 points · What sets them apart

This compares the property itself, which is what separates developments with similar golf settings. Unit count and €/m² are partial indicators: they are no substitute for valuing a particular home.

B1
Architecture and brand
International branded = 10 · National premium = 7 · Considered = 4 · Commercial = 2

Editorial. Architectural authorship and, where there is one, a residential brand. A brand that has been announced but not delivered is capped at 7.

10
B2
Size of the development
≤20 = 10 · 21–50 = 7 · 51–100 = 4 · 101 or more = 2

The whole development counts, not the phase currently for sale. Fewer homes, greater exclusivity.

10
B3
Positioning by €/m²
≥10,000 = 10 · ≥7,000 = 7 · ≥5,000 = 5 · ≥3,500 = 3 · below that = 1

The scale tops out at €10,000: above that, being dearer earns nothing further. Where a figure is an estimate, it is flagged as one.

10
B4
On-site facilities
Spa, gym, beach club, concierge, clubhouse, sports areas

Editorial. Facilities that are documented, not announced. Quantity alone does not set the score.

10

A higher €/m² earns points for positioning; on its own it proves neither better quality nor a better buy. Prices move: confirm them with the developer before you decide anything.

From data to position

How each result is arrived at.

The rules are the same for every development in an edition. What follows is, literally, what this page runs when it loads; each profile shows the points breakdown and the reasoning behind the four editorial criteria.

01 The sum

The score for a development p is the sum of six golf-setting criteria and four development criteria. It is plain addition: ten terms, 100 points.

S(p) = A(p) + B(p) = ∑i=16 ai(p) + ∑j=14 bj(p)0 ≤ A ≤ 60  ·  0 ≤ B ≤ 40
02 Access to the courses

To count nearby courses in A2 and A3, a coefficient is applied according to each course's published access: resort, public, municipal and semi-private count as 1; members-only, 0.5; strictly private, 0. It comes from the 'membership' column of the course database, not from a judgement made development by development. It describes what a visitor can generally expect: it does not certify playing rights included with the property, which are stated separately wherever the developer publishes them.

α(c) = 1 if the course is resort, public, municipal or semi-private  ·  ½ if members-only  ·  0 if strictly private
03 Courses within useful reach

t(p,c) is the driving time measured with OSRM over the OpenStreetMap road network, from the development's coordinate to the clubhouse; s(c) is the course rating (★ to ★★★★). E4 and E3 are the effective density of elite and premium courses: a course you cannot play counts for half, or for nothing.

C15(p) = { c : t(p,c) ≤ 15 min }     E4(p) = ∑c∈C15, s(c)=4 α(c)     E3(p) = ∑c∈C15, s(c)≥3 α(c)
04 The six calculated criteria

A1, A2, A3, A4, B2 and B3 are converted into points by band: the nearest ★★+ course, the effective densities, the driving time, the number of units and the price per square metre. The thresholds are public and identical for every development in an edition. An automatic conversion does not make all of its inputs free of judgement: a course's stars and its access category are classifications decided beforehand, and they are documented under The courses.

c* = arg min { t(p,c) : s(c) ≥ 2 }
a1 = f1(s(c*))★★★★ → 15 · ★★★ → 10 · ★★ → 5
a2 = f2(E4)≥3 → 12 · ≥2 → 8 · ≥1 → 5 · >0 → 2 · 0 → 0
a3 = f3(E3)≥6 → 10 · ≥4 → 7 · ≥2 → 4 · ≥1 → 2 · >0 → 1 · 0 → 0
a4 = f4(t(p,c*))≤3′ → 8 · ≤6′ → 6 · ≤10′ → 4 · ≤15′ → 2 · beyond → 0
b2 = g2(units)≤20 → 10 · ≤50 → 7 · ≤100 → 4 · more → 2
b3 = g3(€/m²)≥10,000 → 10 · ≥7,000 → 7 · ≥5,000 → 5 · ≥3,500 → 3 · less → 1
05 The four editorial criteria

A5, A6, B1 and B4 — integration with the golf, road connections, architecture or brand, and on-site facilities — are worth a maximum of 35 points and each carries its written reasoning, visible on every profile. Two automatic constraints stop promises from scoring: an own course that is not yet open earns nothing, and a brand announced but not delivered is capped at 7. A judgement can be disputed, and overturned with evidence; it is not the same thing as a measurement.

a5, a6, b1, b4∈ editorial, witha5 + a6 + b1 + b4 ≤ 35a5= 0 if the own course is not yet open  · b1≤ 7 if the brand is not yet delivered
06 Order and tie-breaks

Where two scores are level, Block A settles it — the golf setting is sixty of the hundred points and it is what this guide measures first. If Block A is level too, the quality of the benchmark course decides; after that, how close it is. Those four values are what determine a position: when all four match, two developments share it and the next number is skipped. The alphabet breaks no ties; it only fixes the order in which developments sharing a position are displayed.

rank(p) = 1 + |{ q : q ≻ p}|   with q ≻ p ⇔ (Sq, Aq, a1q, −minq) > (Sp, Ap, a1p, −minp)
07 Editions and revisions

Each edition has its own universe of developments, its own data cut-off and its own version of the matrix. The score is a function of that base: prices, unit counts, course openings, changes to access or to driving times. When a change is verified, the whole set is recalculated and published as a dated revision. What you see on screen is the edition named, not live market tracking. This one: edition #01, data closed on 23 September 2026, matrix v1.8; prices gathered in April–May 2026 and driving times measured in August 2026. Next revision: October 2026.

Notes on the method
Why two blocks
  • On distance alone, the same three resorts would win every time.
  • Block B separates developments that are level on location.
  • It rewards architecture, exclusivity and positioning, which is what a prime buyer actually compares.
Why access is weighted
  • Having Valderrama eight minutes away and being able to play it are not the same thing.
  • 7 of the 17 courses rated ★★★+ are private: for anyone who is not a member, they are scenery.
  • Resort, public, municipal and semi-private count in full · members-only, half · strictly private, nothing.
Sources and limits
  • Prices: developer websites, Idealista, Fotocasa and agencies. April–May 2026 — verify before purchase.
  • Courses: at least three sources behind each rating.
  • Driving times: OSRM over OpenStreetMap, with no time-of-day bias; they do not model summer congestion.
  • Coordinates: cross-checked against OpenStreetMap geometry.
What is not taken on trust
  • Only four criteria are matters of judgement: A5, A6, B1 and B4. Each carries its reasoning on the profile.
  • Every figure points to a public source, linked on the final page.
  • Change one figure and the whole ranking is recalculated.
